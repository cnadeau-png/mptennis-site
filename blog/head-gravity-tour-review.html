Head Gravity Tour Review: Something Different, Scored Out of 10

By Cade Nadeau · 6 min read

The short answer: the Gravity Tour feels different from anything I've played in years. It has big power on serve, great comfort (it's a flexible frame) and excellent feel. The weak spots are control when you don't swing fully, and backhand volleys. I liked it enough to play a full set with it, which I rarely do in a playtest.

What stood out

Groundstrokes: when you step on the gas, it goes, and not to the fence. It drives through the court. The teardrop head can feel a bit awkward on the backhand.

Serve: the biggest surprise. More free power than my Extreme Pro, almost Pure Aero territory for me, with great slice and kick.

Comfort: at a 59 stiffness, it flexes a lot. I had no arm pain, and it pairs well with a stiffer string.

Control: on swings that weren't 100%, the ball sometimes sat up or flew long. I didn't have that security blanket feeling.

Volleys: forehand volleys felt great. Backhand volleys were harder to feel.

Who is it for?

A lot of players: club players, college players, competitive juniors. If you have arm issues, the low stiffness is a big plus. I'd recommend a medium to stiff string if you're 4.0 and above. Test it on your own shots before you buy.

FAQ

Is the Head Gravity Tour arm friendly?

Yes. Cade measured it at a low 59 stiffness and had no arm pain. He scored comfort 10 out of 10.

What string pattern is the Head Gravity Tour?

This version is 16x19. The previous Gravity Tour was 18x20.

What is the Head Gravity Tour's weakness?

Control on swings that are not full, and backhand volleys. Cade scored control 6.5 and volleys 7 out of 10.

Who should use the Head Gravity Tour?

Club players, college players and competitive juniors who want power and comfort with good feel.
