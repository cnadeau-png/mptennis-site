Best Tennis Racquets of 2026: I Tested 20. Only 4 I'd Buy (1 I Won't Touch)

By Cade Nadeau · 6 min read

The short answer: I tested over 20 racquets in 2026. My four for club players: #1 Head Boom Pro 98, #2 Head Speed 97 Tour, #3 Wilson Defyer 98 Pro and #4 Wilson Defyer 100. The one I won't touch: the Babolat Pure Aero 98. If you're 3.5 and under, or your arm hurts, start with the Defyer 100.

How I pick a racquet

I've tested hundreds of racquets in the last five years. Before I actually buy one, I need to feel comfortable with it on court. Is it comfortable on the volley? On serve? From the back? Is it stable? Can I add weight? How does it react to different string setups and tensions? If it checks all the boxes, it makes this list.

For most club players, I'd put comfort first, ahead of power and control. That means feeling at home with the racquet, and it means comfort on your arm. That's why racquets like the Wilson Clash have done so well for club players. For me, the Clash wouldn't make my top four, because I don't get that same feeling with it.

#4 Wilson Defyer 100

This is an amazing frame, and it made my top four of the year. It's a 100 square inch racquet, a brand new line from Wilson. If you're looking for a tweener, this needs to be on your demo list. It's very comfortable to use, and you get everything you need at the club level, especially around 4.5 to 5.0, about UTR 7 to 8.

It really surprised me on court. I did not think I was going to enjoy it whatsoever. In my review I liked it more than the 98 in a lot of ways.

The flaw: it would not hold up under pace at higher speeds in match play. I added loads of lead and tried different string setups, and I couldn't crack that code. You might not feel the same issue, and you can still add lead if you need it. For a lot of players I think this racquet is going to be literally insane.

#3 Wilson Defyer 98 Pro

I enjoyed parts of the 100 more. It was more forgiving, and I could take bigger cuts and it just went in more. But the 98 is way more of a player's frame. If I had to take one out to the match court, I'd take the 98, because it handles higher pace better.

You give it power, it lands in. You lay off a little, it still goes in. My friend Gryffin, who's grinding Futures week in and week out, uses the Defyer 98 and loves it. It fits higher performing college players, pros, high level juniors, and 5.0 to 5.5 players.

The flaw: I wish it performed a little better at the net. It felt a little weaker in the hoop at times. It still belongs in the top three.

#2 Head Speed 97 Tour

I really do love this racquet. It's a great player's frame with that traditional Speed feel, but more controlled. It plays well in stock form and even better with a little lead at 3 and 9. Most of the weight on all my Speed Tours was at 3 and 9.

The flaw: the sweet spot is a little small. That's the main reason I stepped away from it to another frame, and I still notice it when I coach with it. I'm hanging on to my Speeds, but this one is for a specific group: 5.5 NTRP and up, about UTR 8.5 and up, with high racquet head speed. If you're below about a 5 UTR, this is not a great frame for you unless you're swinging really fast.

#1 Head Boom Pro 98

It completely shocked me. I did not think I was going to like this racquet as much as I did. I really didn't love the previous versions of the Boom. I've been using it for the last couple of months, and it holds up.

I can enjoy a frame through the demo process, but there's an extra set of criteria to play at a high level in matches. This one checked all the boxes. The standout: it holds up against really high ball speeds with not that much lead. I have standard lead tape a little higher than 10 and 2, right where my fingers are. Nothing in the butt.

I can counterpunch really well, defend really well, and flatten out the ball when I need to. I've got great feel, I can knife a nice slice, and my volleys feel super crisp.

The flaw: serves took me a couple of days to get used to, because of where the weight sits compared to my Speed Tours. After a few days of serving, no issues at all. If you're adding or taking away weight, give it a few days.

Unlike the Speed Tour, the Boom Pro is going to work for a much larger audience, because the sweet spot is super generous. It doesn't feel like a 98. It feels bigger, and it reminds me of the Defyer 100 that way. I'm not shooting balls off the fence either. I played with it this morning and thought, I can drop my tension even more.

It's a great frame for a 4.5 club player, a high level junior or a college player who doesn't want an ultra control frame like a Prestige or Pro Staff, but isn't after a Pure Aero or Pure Drive either. I actually ordered more of these racquets.

Honorable mention: Babolat Pure Aero 100

It didn't make the list, but I enjoyed playing with it, and that frame still works for so many players. The Pure Aero line has done so well for so many years.

The bust of the year: Babolat Pure Aero 98

This racquet failed so miserably. It has so much potential. It should be the best racquet on the market for college players, Futures players and high level club players, anyone above about UTR 8 or 4.5.

With a 98, I'd expect a little more control than the 100. I didn't even feel that in my review. It felt like a stiff board, which is not a great sensation if you're playing with a Pure Aero. Now, with the Defyer 98, Wilson kind of stole Babolat's thunder.

Nobody pays me to make these videos. I was extremely disappointed with this frame, and the previous versions were way better. If you want a Pure Aero 98, go with a previous version and watch for an update. It's not just that I didn't like it. This racquet has the potential to be the best on the market for high level players.
