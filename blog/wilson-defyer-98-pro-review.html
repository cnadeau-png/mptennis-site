Wilson Defyer 98 Pro Review: One Flaw. Everything Else Is Perfect

By Cade Nadeau · 5 min read

The short answer: the Wilson Defyer 98 Pro is the everything frame. Big sweet spot for a 98, easy depth, control when you swing fast, and flat serves that feel unbelievable. The one flaw is low volleys below the waist, where it feels a tiny bit weak. A little lead tape should fix it. It was a top two racquet of the year for me.

This review is from August 2026, after taking real time with the frame.

The everything frame

If I was telling someone about this racquet for the first time, I'd say this is the everything frame. Power, control and spin, with lots of stability, while still being maneuverable and light. Wilson aced it. I honestly think this is the new standard every brand has to chase.

It's a 16x20 pattern and a 305, so it's not heavy. It's going up against the Head Extremes, the VCOREs and the Aero 98, and in my eyes it's the frontrunner by a lot.

A huge sweet spot for a 98

You feel something different from the first hit. If you're stuck between a 98 and a 100, you don't even have to worry about it. You can hit outside the sweet spot and still hit a quality ball, from the short court or the baseline.

You get great depth and power without hitting the ball off the fence. Frames like the Pure Aero and Pure Drive are made for a certain type of player, and I struggled with them. This one doesn't have that downside.

From the baseline

The faster you swing, the more control you get. The slower you swing, the more depth you get. There's no losing.

I hit a flat backhand, and with this racquet my net clearance was about a foot and a half higher right away. I don't get that with any other frame. On the run it's the same deal.

You can play flat or heavy spin and switch whenever you want. Aeros want to give you spin all the time. With this one you can turn it on and off. It still has that traditional Wilson feel too. Slice is above average, and returning flat serves was no issue at all.

Serve

By far, this racquet feels the most alive. Flat serves are unbelievable. The ball moves through the court, I hit my spots, and with a new can of balls it sounds like a gunshot. Kicks bounced nice and high, and I could pull players off the court with the slice wide.

The one flaw: low volleys

Put away volleys, punch volleys and anything above the waist: no issues at all. The only knock: balls below the waist, where I had to hit up to get deep, or very touch oriented shots where I needed the racquet to hold its angle. It felt a tiny bit weak there.

I think a little lead tape would fix it. I hadn't tried it yet because I didn't want it to change my review. A lot of people won't even notice this.

String setup

I played it with a full bed of Restring Sync in 18 gauge, because I ran out of 17. I really liked it. I might like the 17 even more.

The verdict

One flaw, and everything else is perfect. This was a top two racquet of the year for me, maybe even first. Wilson didn't pay me to say that. Some people won't like it, and that's fine. But I really feel this is the future of racquets.

FAQ

Is the Wilson Defyer 98 Pro good for club players?

Yes. It works for intermediate and advanced club players, weekend warriors, college and pro players. The sweet spot is big for a 98, so you get easy depth without hitting the ball long.

What is the Wilson Defyer 98 Pro's weakness?

Low volleys and touch shots below the waist. It can feel a tiny bit weak when you need the racquet to hold its angle. A little lead tape should fix it.

Is the Defyer 98 Pro heavy?

No. It is a 305 and very maneuverable, with a 16x20 string pattern.

What strings did you use in the Defyer 98 Pro?

A full bed of Restring Sync in 18 gauge. I really liked it, and the 17 gauge might be even better.
