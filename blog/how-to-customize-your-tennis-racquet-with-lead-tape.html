How to Customize Your Tennis Racquet with Lead Tape (A Complete Guide)

By MPTennis · 7 min read

Customizing your tennis racquet is one of the most underrated things you can do for your game. Most players use a stock racquet straight off the shelf — but the players who really dial in their setup are adding lead tape in the right spots, and they notice an immediate difference in how the racquet feels and performs.

This is exactly what we do with our own racquets and what we teach through the Ace Tennis Coach app. Here's a complete walkthrough of the basics.

Why Customize At All?

Every racquet comes from the manufacturer with a stock weight and balance. But stock specs are a starting point — not necessarily the best setup for your game. Your swing speed, grip style, playing style, and even your specific frame can all benefit from targeted customization.

Lead tape is cheap, removable, and easy to experiment with. You can add a gram, test it on court, and pull it off if it doesn't work. It's the most practical way to fine-tune a racquet without buying a new one.

Stock vs. Customized Specs

When we weighed a stock Head Extreme Tour, it came in at 301g — slightly under the advertised 305g spec. Not unusual. Quality control variations of 3–5 grams are common across all brands. After full customization with lead and strings, that same frame came in at 335g.

That 34-gram difference changed how the racquet played entirely — more stability, more plow-through, a different balance point. That's the power of intentional customization.

Lead Tape Positions and What They Do

12 o'clock — Power and head-heavy feel

Adding lead at the very top of the racquet increases swingweight and makes the frame more head-heavy. This is the position that adds the most power and the most weight through the hitting zone.

Use this if: you want more plow-through, your shots lack depth, or you want to feel more weight through contact.

Watch out: too much at 12 makes the racquet feel sluggish and harder to accelerate. Start with 1–2 grams and test before adding more.

3 & 9 o'clock — Stability

Lead at 3 and 9 (the sides of the hoop) is the go-to position for stability. If your racquet twists on off-center hits or gets pushed around on hard shots, this is where to start. It adds swingweight without dramatically changing the balance. We run about 4.5 cm of lead on each side at 3 and 9 on the Head Speed Tour.

Use this if: your shots lack stability, you play against hard hitters, or you feel the frame twisting on volleys and off-center groundstrokes.

10 & 2 o'clock — The hybrid position

This is our favorite addition to the 3 & 9 setup. Lead at 10 and 2 sits between the top and the sides, giving you a blend of stability and power. You get more head weight than 3 & 9 alone, but without the full sluggishness of 12 o'clock.

On the Extreme Tour setup, we ran 9 cm of lead at 10 and 2 combined with 4.5 cm at 3 and 9 — a combination dialed in over several weeks of on-court testing.

Use this if: you want more weight in the hoop without going full 12 o'clock, or you're after that stability-plus-punch combination.

Handle — Head-light feel and maneuverability

Adding weight to the handle under the replacement grip makes the racquet more head-light. It increases maneuverability and makes the frame feel faster through the air — but it also reduces plow-through.

We ran about 9 cm of lead on each side under the grip in the handle area on the Extreme. Combined with the hoop weight, the overall frame is heavier but the balance stays manageable.

Use this if: your racquet feels too head-heavy, you want a quicker feel, or you've added hoop weight and need to bring the balance back.

Throat — Static weight only

Adding weight in the throat (the V-shaped section between handle and hoop) mostly increases static weight without significantly changing balance or swingweight. We've tried it and removed it — it made the frame feel heavier in the hand without the benefits of hoop weight. Generally not recommended unless you have a very specific reason.

How Much to Add

Start small. Add 1–2 grams at a position, hit with it for a full session, then decide. Lead tape has a bigger impact than most players expect — a single gram at 12 o'clock can meaningfully change how the racquet feels through contact.
