Lower String Tension: Why I Tried 44 lbs, and What It Does

By Cade Nadeau · 5 min read

The short answer: stringing lower (roughly under 50 lbs with a polyester string) can give you more power, more comfort on your arm, a bigger sweet spot and more spin. I strung Restring Sync at 44 lbs and really saw the benefits. You don't have to copy my number. Drop a few pounds from where you are and see what changes.

Why tensions used to be so high

For decades, top players used natural gut, often strung tight. That's why high tensions stuck around. Once polyester strings took over in the early 2000s, tensions didn't need to be that high anymore, and a lot of players started going lower.

Pros who string low

Some players go really low. Adrian Mannarino has strung somewhere around 18 to 22 lbs. Jack Sock was known for the low 40s, sometimes the upper 30s. Daniil Medvedev sits around 46 to 48 lbs. You don't need to go that far. It just shows low tensions work at the highest level.

4 benefits of a lower tension

  1. More power. The string bed has more give, so the ball rebounds with more energy.
  2. More comfort. Easier on your wrist, elbow and shoulder. A stiff poly at a lower tension can feel much friendlier.
  3. A bigger sweet spot. More forgiveness when you don't hit the center, which means more consistency.
  4. More spin. The strings move and snap back more, which gives you more spin potential.

My test: Restring Sync at 44 lbs

Sync is a string usually strung higher, and stiff polys like it can cause arm pain for some players. At 44 lbs, I had no problems in that area. The ball moved through the court way more, and my hitting partner noticed the difference too.

I tested the 17 gauge (1.30). For me, the thinner 18 gauge would play even better.

How to try a lower tension

  • Drop a few pounds from your current tension, not 10 at once.
  • Keep the same string so you know the tension is what changed.
  • Play a few sessions and points before you decide.
  • If balls start flying long, you went too low. Go back up a bit.

An update: today I usually string in the low 50s with Slap and Sync in my Boom Pro 98. The right tension depends on your racquet, your string and your swing. Find your fit, then stop shopping.

FAQ

Does lower string tension give you more power?

Yes. A lower tension lets the string bed give more, so the ball rebounds with more energy.

Is lower string tension better for tennis elbow?

It can help. Lower tension is more comfortable on the wrist, elbow and shoulder, especially with a stiff polyester string.

Does lower tension give you more spin?

Usually. The strings move and snap back more at lower tensions, which gives you more spin potential.

What tension should I try?

Drop a few pounds from what you use now and test it. Cade tested Restring Sync at 44 lbs, and today usually strings in the low 50s.
