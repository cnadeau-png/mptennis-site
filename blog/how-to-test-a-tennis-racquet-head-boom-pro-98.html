How to Test a Tennis Racquet Before You Switch (Head Boom Pro 98)

By Cade Nadeau · 5 min read

The short answer: a racquet can feel great in practice and fall apart in a match. Before I switch, it has to pass three stages: hitting, then points, then a practice set. And it has to pass two checks: does it hold up under pressure, and do I like how the ball comes off the strings? The Head Boom Pro 98 passed. But if you're 4.0 and under, the racquet isn't what's holding you back.

My Boom Pro 98 setup

I switched to the Boom Pro 98 about a month ago, when I started my new coaching job. In my first review, the only thing I felt it lacked was a little stability in the hoop. So I added a bit of lead just above 3 and 9, almost at 10 and 2. That's where it felt weak on returns and some volleys.

Why a few days of hitting isn't a test

When I review racquets on the channel, I start with hitting. At my level, that tells me a lot. But switching to a frame is different. That process takes way longer than a few days.

Think of it like a product. Before you release it, it has to pass a lot of tests. For a racquet, the test goes:

  1. Hitting
  2. Points
  3. A practice set or match

My two instant fail checks

1. Does it hold up? Stability is huge. If a racquet is buckling on a big serve or on the run, it's the worst feeling there is. That's the number one thing for me.

2. How does it come off the strings? Every racquet feels a little different. Do I like that feeling?

The serve matters too. If a racquet isn't working on my serve under pressure, it's an immediate no.

Testing it on the shots my game is built on

I tested it in points on the shots I rely on: my forehand inside out, my slice, defending from the corners, and my serve.

  • Forehand inside out: it's point and shoot. When I tell it to do something, it does it.
  • Slice: it stays nice and low over the net, and I can feel the ball knifing on the string bed.
  • Defending: when I'm on the run, I'm not wondering if the racquet will hold up. You should only be thinking about what you're doing with the ball.

Then I played a short set against Mark, who coaches with me. He played Nationals for years and played for Nova Scotia at the Canada Games.

Did it pass?

Yes. When the score counted, I didn't think about the racquet once. That's usually a good sign. Win or lose, did you think about the racquet? Did it hurt you? It didn't. I never thought, "I could have made that with a different racquet."

It's the first racquet I've really trusted since my early college days.

How to test your racquet

Your game isn't my game. Somebody else might try the Boom Pro and spray it off the back fence. That's fine. Maybe your racquet is a Pro Staff or a Pure Aero. Here's how to test on your shots before you spend the money:

  1. Get a demo and hit with it.
  2. Play points with it, not just rallies.
  3. Test the situations you play in matches. A particular shot, a pattern, second serves to the backhand. If you're not hitting the shots you win points with, you could end up with a racquet that doesn't perform when it matters.
  4. Play practice sets against different players. Not everyone hits the same. How does it play against someone faster? Slower?

The truth for 4.0 and under

If you're a weekend warrior, a club player or 4.0 and under, it's not the racquet. So many people blame the racquet when they need to look in the mirror.

I'm playing the Boom Pro right now. It feels good. Maybe I'll play a little better. But it's not going to take me to the next level. A racquet can complement your game. It won't define it.

So go work on your game, and find a racquet that works for you. Find your fit, then stop shopping.

FAQ

How do you test a tennis racquet before buying it?

Start with hitting, then play points, then play practice sets against different players. Test it on the shots you rely on in matches, and ask two questions: does it hold up under pressure, and do you like how the ball comes off the strings?

Will a new racquet make me a better player?

For most club players, 4.0 and under, it's not the racquet. The right one can complement your game, but it won't take you to the next level.

What strings does Cade use in the Boom Pro 98?

Restring Slap in the mains and Restring Sync in the crosses, usually in the low 50s.

How long should you demo a racquet?

A first hit tells you a lot, but switching takes longer than a few days. Play points and practice sets with it against players who hit differently before you decide.
