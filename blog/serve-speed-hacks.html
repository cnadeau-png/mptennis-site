Serve Speed Hacks: 4 Quick Fixes to Add MPH to Your Serve

By Cade Nadeau · 4 min read

Ever feel like your serve is stuck in slow motion? There are a few common hiccups that might be holding you back. Here are four quick fixes that can add serious mph to your serve — whether you're a seasoned player or just getting started.

1. Grip — Get a Grip

To rev up your serve, make sure you're using a continental grip. Using a forehand grip? Time to switch it up. A continental grip helps you get your racquet on edge, creating that crucial racquet head speed.

One way to think about how the racquet should feel in your hands: imagine how you would hold a hammer when hammering in a nail. That's the continental grip.

2. Keep Your Palm Down — The Golden Rule

During your serve motion, keep that palm down until you're ready to unleash at the ball during contact. It might feel a bit odd at first, but it makes a world of difference.

The picture of Roger Federer below is a perfect example — his palm and strings are pointing down as he arrives in his trophy pose. This allows for a better transition to keeping the racquet on edge and increases racquet head speed.

3. Serve Like You Throw — Mimic the Motion

Ever noticed how your throwing motion is similar to your serve? Capture that moment just before you throw a ball and you've got a glimpse of the ideal trophy position. It's all about that fluid, natural movement.

To develop the perfect serve kinetic chain, understand the mechanics of a throw before adding any jump. By staying grounded it becomes easier to develop the throwing technique and understand how to effectively use your body.

4. Grip Pressure — Find Your Sweet Spot

On a scale from 0 to 10 (with 10 being a death grip), aim for a grip pressure of 4 to 6. Too tight and you risk injury and reduced racquet head speed. Experiment within this range and discover what works best for your serve.

Grip pressure in tennis is dynamic — it adapts to different situations and strokes. Understanding the appropriate grip pressure for each scenario is vital for controlling the ball in both easy and difficult situations.

Put It Together

These four tweaks work together. The continental grip gives you the right tool. The palm-down position sets you up to use it. The throwing motion creates the kinetic chain. And loose grip pressure lets all of that actually happen at full speed.

Work on these over the next few weeks and you might just see an extra 3 to 5 mph added to your serve. It's all about progress, not perfection.
