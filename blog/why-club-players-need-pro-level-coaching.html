Why club players deserve pro-level coaching

By Cade Nadeau · 5 min read

Walk into most clubs in Canada on a Tuesday night and you'll see the same thing: a coach feeding balls from a hopper while four adults take turns hitting forehands they already know how to hit. Everyone leaves sweaty, nobody leaves better.

That's not coaching. That's supervised exercise.

The assumption nobody questions

The unspoken rule in club tennis is that pro-level coaching — the kind that wins on the ATP tour — belongs to elite juniors and college recruits. Club players get cardio drills, occasional technique tips, and a vague suggestion to "play more matches."

I think that's backwards.

Pros already know what to do. They've been taught. The advantage they have isn't a secret forehand — it's a thousand small tactical decisions per match that the average club player never gets exposed to.

  • How to read a server's toss in the first three games.
  • When to break pattern, not when to repeat one.
  • What to do on the first point after losing a game.
  • Which side of the court your opponent's confidence lives on.

None of that requires UTR 11. All of it makes you better tomorrow.

What we actually teach

The MPTennis system is the same system we used to get Cade to college and pro tennis. We don't water it down for club players — we just translate it.

A 4.0 club player doesn't need a different theory of tennis than a college captain. They need the same theory, applied to their level, with their racquet, against the kind of opponents they actually face on Saturday morning.

The gap between a 3.5 and a 4.5 isn't talent. It's tactical literacy. We close that gap on purpose.

Why it works for adults

Club players have something juniors don't: they listen. They've been around the game long enough to know what's not working. Give them a real reason and a real plan, and the improvement curve is steep.

The other thing club players have is patience. They're not chasing a ranking deadline. That means we can teach the patterns that actually hold up under pressure — not the trick shots that look great on YouTube and disappear in a real match.

The takeaway

You don't need a different coach because you're a club player. You need the same coach a serious junior would have, just pointed at your game.

That's what we built MPTennis to be.
