Head Boom Pro 98 Review: I Hated It at First. Now It's in My Bag

By Cade Nadeau · 5 min read

The short answer: the Head Boom Pro 98 is a 98 that feels like a 100. It's forgiving, I can swing 100% freely without spraying, and it serves great. The only flaw for me is it's a little head light, which a bit of lead at 10 and 2 fixes. It's a great fit for juniors, college players and anyone moving down from a 100. Skip it if you're 3.0 and under.

Why I hated it at first

My first 15 to 20 minutes with this frame, I didn't want to play with it. I was testing it alongside the Wilson Defyer 100 on clay, and these racquets play completely different. Side by side, they just didn't match.

So I took it out on its own and gave it real time. That changed everything. It's been in my bag ever since.

A 98 that feels like a 100

The head is a little wider than a traditional shape. It's not quite like a Yonex either. That makes it really forgiving. Off center hits almost don't feel like they exist with this frame.

The best part: it doesn't hurt maneuverability. The beam is thin, so it swings nicely through the air. You get the bigger sweet spot of a 100 with the benefits of a 98.

Swing out without spraying

It gives you power like a Speed, spin like an Extreme, and good touch on top of that. I actually like it more than the Speed Pro.

The big thing for me: I can swing 100% freely and the ball isn't going off the fence. With more power frames I get nervous I might spray. Here I get the control I need, and great directional control. I can feel the ball coming in and out of the string bed.

The one flaw: stability

For me it's a little too head light. I wanted a tiny bit more stability on contact, on a return or a ball that comes through the court with pace. So I'm adding a little weight, closer to 10 and 2 than 3 and 9.

This is a very minor thing. I've played multiple times and forgot to add the lead, and it was no big deal. On the Defyer I put lead on right away because I felt it needed it. Most club players won't even notice this, and a lot of people won't need to add weight at all. Slices and volleys feel really good.

String setup

I'm running Slap in the mains and Sync in the crosses. It pockets really well for a poly poly hybrid, so I think most string setups will work well in this frame.

Serve

Very good. No stability issues on serve, I can hit my spots, and the spin production is great. A firmer setup like a full bed of Sync might help me hit my spots even more, but that's me being super analytical.

The verdict in one word

Complete, or balanced. It doesn't do one thing amazing, but it does everything really well, and I feel comfortable with it. I could play a match with it. It might even sneak into my top five of the year.

Update: I did switch. I added a little lead in the hoop, kept the Slap and Sync hybrid, and then tested it in real points. Read the test a racquet has to pass before I switch.

FAQ

Is the Head Boom Pro 98 forgiving?

Yes. The head is a little wider than a traditional 98, so it feels like a 100. Off center hits barely feel like they exist, and it still swings easily through the air.

Does the Head Boom Pro 98 need lead tape?

Most people will not need it. For me it was a little head light, so I added a bit of weight closer to 10 and 2 than 3 and 9 for more stability on returns and fast balls.

Who is the Head Boom Pro 98 for?

High performing juniors, college players, and players moving down from a 100 to a 98. If you are 3.0 and under, there are better options in the Boom line.

What strings work in the Boom Pro 98?

I use Slap mains and Sync crosses. It pockets well as a poly poly hybrid, so most string setups will work in this frame.
