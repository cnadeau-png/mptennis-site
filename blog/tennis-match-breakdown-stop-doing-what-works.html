I Lost 6-2, 6-3 to a Former D1 Player. Here's What Decided It.

By Cade Nadeau · 6 min read

The short answer: when I came to the net, I won 71% of the points (12 of 17). It got me on the board from 0-5. Then, as soon as the score got closer, I stopped doing it. Club players do the same thing every week: you find what works, then go back to your habits when it gets tight. If something works, keep doing it.

The match

Gryffin is a good buddy of mine. We've played each other for years. He's a former D1 player with a 12 UTR, and his level has really gone up over the last year and a half. His ball moves through the court fast, and that's something I've always struggled with.

This was indoors on hard court. We hadn't played indoors the whole tournament. The plan going in: take his time away and come forward. That's always worked against him.

Was it the racquet?

Gryffin switched from the Head Radical MP to the Wilson Defyer 98 a few weeks before this match. I was playing my Head Speed Tour 97 with Restring Sync.

The Defyer was the right switch for him. That extra 3% to 5% of ball quality over two hours makes a difference. My Speed Tour has a small sweet spot, and it can crumble a little when balls come outside of it.

But was I beaten by the racquet or by Gryffin? Absolutely by Gryffin. Did his racquet make a difference? Of course. Does mine? Yes. But that's not what decided it.

Down 0-5: the plan finally kicks in

Right out of the gate, Gryffin came out hot. I wasn't mentally overwhelmed. I was tennis overwhelmed. I played way too much slice backhand, gave up court, and didn't play over the ball.

In my first service game, I came forward twice and won both points. But I didn't keep doing it. I figured it out too late.

At 0-5, I told myself to go to the net literally every point, even if I didn't want to. I won the next two games.

Serving at 2-5: why I stopped

Then I stopped. I knew it was working, but I had a fear that he knew it was coming. I thought I needed to give him a different look. I missed two plus ones and lost the game, and the set 6-2.

In hindsight, I should have kept going to the net. That's how I got on the board.

My dad's view from the fence: when you can't hit through the court with pace, the other way to take time away is to come to the net. It was working. That was the play.

Set 2: up a break, then broken

In the second set I broke to go up 3-2. I was hitting through my backhand more, and if I'd played slice on that pass, I would have lost the point.

Serving at 3-2, I was still trying to apply pressure. But I missed two short balls when it got tight, and Gryffin ran away with the rest of the set, 6-3. I was coming in. I just didn't execute.

Gryffin didn't come to the net much because he didn't have to. I was making the errors. If I come in 30 times instead of 17 and win 70% of those, maybe this match looks different.

My first serve went in 63% of the time, but I only won 50% of those points. That means I'm not doing enough with the plus one. Too many times I'm fighting to be neutral. It needs to be like a punch, not waiting for them to punch.

The club player version

You don't have to play a former D1 player to make these mistakes. I see it all the time at club team tennis.

  1. Be self aware. A lot of club players have no idea what's actually happening in the match. Ask: What am I doing well? What am I not doing well? What are they doing well? What aren't they?
  2. If you find something that works, keep doing it. Until your opponent finds an answer. Don't drop it because the score got closer.
  3. Lock in on the big points. 30-all, 40-30, 30-40 and deuce still count as one point, but they matter more than 15-love.
  4. Get creative on short balls. You don't have to hit it hard. Use an angle to pull them off the court, so they have to hit up on the next ball, and you're at the net.

Then go practice what cost you points. It's a loop: play, review, practice, play again. Play smarter, not more.

What I'm playing for the rematch

I've been playing the Head Boom Pro 98 with a little lead at 10 and 2, and Slap and Sync strings. I don't have the same sweet spot issues I had with the Speed Tour. And I've been training with Gryffin a lot, so a rematch on the channel might happen.

FAQ

Why do tennis players stop doing what's working?

Usually fear or habit. When the score gets closer, you worry they've figured it out and go back to your comfort zone. If it's working, keep doing it until they find an answer.

What should a club player do with a short ball?

You don't need to hit it hard. Use an angle to pull your opponent off the court, so they have to hit up on the next ball, and follow it to the net.

How do you get better after a loss?

Look at what you did well and what you didn't, then go practice that. Play, review, practice, play again.

Did the Wilson Defyer 98 win the match?

No. I was beaten by Gryffin, not the racquet. The Defyer gives him a little extra help, but patterns and decisions decided it.
